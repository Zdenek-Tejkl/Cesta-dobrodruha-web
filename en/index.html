15 DAYS · ONE BIG COUNTER-CLOCKWISE LOOP

Where we are going

We land in Marrakesh but we do not sleep there; we drive straight to the ocean, because we want to begin with the sea, not with the busiest city in the country. Marrakesh waits for the end, when you know what to shop for and the morning airport is close. We fly direct from Prague.

DAYS 1–4 · ANZA AND AGADIR

We start at the ocean

Straight from the airport to the Atlantic: four nights in one house by the ocean. Three days of surf lessons with an instructor, dinosaur footprints within walking distance of the house, and the Agadir markets, where local women press argan oil.

DAY 5 · ESSAOUIRA

Harbour, ramparts, snails

A blue-and-white port town. You pick your fish straight from a crate at the market, plus babbouche, snail soup from a street stall. Most people do not dare; most of those who do order a second one. We sleep in a riad inside the ramparts.

DAY 6 · CASABLANCA

A day about Islam

The Hassan II Mosque, its minaret rising 210 metres above the Atlantic, one of the few mosques in Morocco open to non-Muslims; our tour is booked. Then an hour of context: the five pillars and why the call to prayer sounds. In the evening, a tented camp by the Merja Zerga lagoon.

DAYS 7–8 · CHEFCHAOUEN

The blue city without the crowds

From the lagoon up a winding road into the Rif mountains. We arrive for sunset, and in the morning the blue alleys are ours alone, before the buses come.

DAYS 8–9 · VOLUBILIS AND FEZ

We sleep inside the medina

On the way, the holy town of Moulay Idriss and the Roman mosaics of Volubilis in the afternoon light. Then two nights in a riad right in the Fez medina: nine thousand alleys, a full day with a local guide, the Chouara tanneries and a hammam in the evening.

DAY 10 · MIDDLE ATLAS

Cedars, monkeys and a night in the mountains

A cedar forest with wild Barbary macaques, mint tea in the mountain town of Ifrane and a night in Midelt at 1,500 metres. The easiest day of the second half; tonight is when the warm layer comes out.

DAY 11 · ZIZ GORGE AND ERG CHEBBI

By evening we enter the desert

The Ziz gorge and mud-brick ksars on the way south. We set out for the camp in the dunes before sunset: by camel or by quad bike with an instructor, your choice, same price. Dinner by the fire and a night under the stars.

DAY 12 · RISSANI AND ALNIF

Sunrise, a nomad market and a trilobite of your own

Sunrise over the dunes and camels back again. The Sunday market in Rissani, where the whole region comes to trade, and the fields near Alnif, where locals lend you a hammer: find a fossil 400 million years old and you take home something that money cannot buy. We sleep in a camp in the Draa valley.

DAYS 13–15 · DRAA · AIT BEN HADDOU · MARRAKESH

The grand finale

The Draa valley, the mud-brick Ait Ben Haddou known from Gladiator and the Tizi n’Tichka pass. Two days in Marrakesh: the medina with a local guide, the big shopping run and a hammam. Early on day fifteen, we fly home.

The skeleton of the itinerary is fixed: the places we sleep and the tickets we have paid for do not change. Inside each day there is room: when a conversation starts at the market, we are in no hurry.

Where we sleep and what we eat

Wherever we can, we rent a whole riad or a small hotel just for our group: no strangers, and in the evening the courtyard is ours. Rooms are shared, for two to four people; if you travel alone, you room with someone of the same sex at no extra charge. Three nights are under canvas: one by a lagoon on the coast, one in the Erg Chebbi dunes and one in the Draa valley. It is not a European hotel standard. It is better: it is the real Morocco.

Food is included the whole time: breakfasts, lunches and dinners. We eat where the locals eat: tagine, couscous, harira, fish straight from the market. Vegetarian and other diets are no problem when we know in advance. You only pay for your own alcohol.

Is it for you?

Come if…

  • you have done Europe and want something bolder, but not entirely alone
  • you travel solo and do not mind a shared room
  • you want to see how people really live, not just the sights
  • you can handle the plan changing now and then
  • you are curious about food, faith, crafts and people

Do not come if…

  • you want to lie by a pool with all inclusive
  • you need every hour set in stone
  • you cannot stand heat, dust and longer drives
  • you expect European hotel standards
  • you will not respect local customs in dress and behaviour
